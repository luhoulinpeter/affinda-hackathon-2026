# 🎪 The challenge

**Fieldday Events** is a five-person events company that runs club nights and small gigs across Melbourne. The City of Melbourne wanted a new summer festival for young people, and Fieldday's founder, Jess, pitched for it. Her run of sold-out nights won the City over, and Fieldday was awarded **Riverside**: a three-day riverfront festival in mid-December, with up to 15,000 people a day.

To get the City over the line, Jess agreed to one condition. If Fieldday isn't on track two weeks out, it pays for an experienced production company to take over. Riverside will go ahead either way. But Fieldday now has ten weeks to deliver **60 artists, 40 food vendors, 300 volunteers and 15,000 people a day** with five staff, and paying for a takeover would wipe out the company.

**This is where you come in.** You're a friend of Jess's, and you know how to build with AI. She's asked you to show her, in 48 hours, how AI could let her team pull this off.

<aside>
🎯

**Your challenge:** Build an AI-powered product that helps Fieldday successfully deliver Riverside at a scale they've never operated before.

</aside>

### The one-sentence version

Pick one of three people (or groups) at Riverside, find a real moment where things go wrong for them, and build a product where AI does something that wasn't possible before.

## 💡 What you can assume

- **No coding experience needed.** No-code tools, AI coding tools and vibe coding are all welcome. We judge the product and the thinking, not the code. New to AI tools? Start with the AI Starter Pack.
- **Start fresh.** Fieldday has no existing systems you need to plug into.
- **Go narrow.** You only need to solve one problem well, not run the whole festival.
- **AI should do real work** in your product, not just sit on top of it.
- **Fill in the details.** Anything the brief doesn't say, you can decide, as long as it's realistic.
- **Use AI to build, not to think for you.** The ideas and decisions should be your own: judges will ask you about them.

## 🧭 Skip the obvious first idea

When most people hear "AI for a festival", they reach for one of two things: a festival chatbot that answers questions, or a dashboard that flags problems. Both are fine starting points, and you won't lose marks just for building one. But they're the idea most teams will have first, and they rarely make the most of what AI can do. The strongest entries start from a specific person in a specific moment, and ask what would genuinely change their day.

# 🛤️ The three tracks

Each track is a different person (or group) to build for. Every track uses the same brief and the same judging criteria. Open a track for the full guide.

### 🎤 Track 1: Backstage

*Artist & vendor operations* · **Building for:** Jess and Ravi, the production manager

Sixty artists and forty food vendors, each with their own needs, paperwork and last-minute changes, and one production manager holding it all together. Build something that keeps every requirement straight and catches problems before they reach the stage or the site.

Track 1: Backstage

### 🎟️ Track 2: Front Row

*The festival-goer experience* · **Building for:** festival-goers, up to 15,000 a day

A bigger and broader crowd than Fieldday has ever looked after: first-timers, families, people with disabilities, people who don't speak much English. Build something that makes the day noticeably better for the people in it, without five staff looking after everyone one at a time.

Track 2: Front Row

### 🦺 Track 3: Ground Control

*Crew & safety operations* · **Building for:** Mo, the safety lead, and the volunteer coordinator

Three hundred volunteers, a hot December weekend and incidents that don't wait. Build something that gets the right people to the right place and helps Mo respond when plans change. Every decision about people's safety stays with a person.

Track 3: Ground Control

# 📤 What to submit

### Stage 1 · on Devpost by Thursday 8 October, 5:00pm

1. **A working prototype:** a link, or clear steps to try it. Any tools are fine, including no-code and AI coding tools. It doesn't need to be production-ready.
2. **A demo video, up to 5 minutes,** showing the product working for its user.
3. **A short description:** the problem, who it's for, what you built, how AI is used, and the tools you used.
4. **Optional:** a GitHub repo, slides.

### Stage 2 · finalists, Friday 9 October at Closing Night

A live 5-minute pitch and demo, then questions from the judges. Slides encouraged. Finalists must attend in person.

For more details, see: https://affinda-challenge.devpost.com/

# ⚖️ How you'll be judged

Every track is judged on the same six criteria, scored out of 100. The same rubric is used for screening and for the finals.

| Criterion | Points | The question judges ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🎯 **Problem understanding** | 15 | Did you find a real, specific problem for a clear user? |
| 🧩 **Product thinking** | 15 | Did you make deliberate choices about what to build, and what to leave out? |
| 🤖 **Use of AI** | 25 | Does AI do meaningful work, with a person in control where it matters? |
| 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 **User experience** | 15 | Would the user actually use this, in their real situation? |
| ✨ **Originality** | 15 | Did you go beyond the obvious first idea? |
| 🚀 **Execution and communication** | 15 | Does it work, and can you show and explain it clearly? |
